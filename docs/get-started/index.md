# Meet PaperSeek

PaperSeek is an open-source literature research workspace. Start with a question, discover relevant papers, follow their citation connections, and organize the evidence into a review.

It combines language models with scholarly data sources. You can inspect the search strategy and progress, open original records, and export a reading list.

## Choose your edition

Use PaperSeek online, or run the community edition on your own computer or server. Choose the option that fits your work and start your first search.

### How do the two options compare?

Search, results, citation maps, bibliometrics, and reviews work the same way in both; the difference is where your data lives and whose models you use.

| | PaperSeek online | Community edition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Start | Open [paperseek.app](https://paperseek.app/) | Install on your computer or server |
| Models | PaperSeek Service with daily credits, or your own keys | Your own model services, including local endpoints |
| History | Your signed-in account | Local database and files |
| Account | Required for saved history and reviews | Not required |
| Model cost | Free daily credits during the public beta | Your own provider accounts |

Choose **online** to start researching now without installing anything. Choose **Community** to keep data on your own machine, use local or private model endpoints, or work without an account. You can switch later — the workflow is the same.

## A complete research workflow

1. **Describe the question.** Include the subject, relationship, and context that matter.
2. **Search and refine.** PaperSeek plans and iterates source-specific queries.
3. **Inspect the papers.** Read abstracts, relevance explanations, metadata, and source links.
4. **Explore connections.** With OpenAlex citation expansion, look beyond the initial results.
5. **Develop a review.** Select papers, approve an outline, and draft from their abstract evidence.

## What to expect

PaperSeek helps discover and organize literature. It does not guarantee an exhaustive systematic search or replace reading original papers. A relevance score is not a study-quality assessment. The review feature uses available abstracts and metadata, not automatically retrieved full text.

Your source, model, budget, and optional ranking stages affect coverage, latency, and provider usage.
